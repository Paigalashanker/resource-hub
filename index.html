01 BEFORE THE JOURNEY

I was never the person who had everything figured out.

I grew up with questions. A lot of them.

Questions about technology. Questions about life. Questions about what I could become.

Sometimes I would sit quietly and imagine a future that didn't exist yet. A future where I could build things.

Websites. Robots. AI systems. Things that people could actually use.

I didn't know exactly how I would get there. I only knew one thing:

"I WANTED TO BUILD SOMETHING OF MY OWN."
02 THE BOY WHO KEPT THINKING

I have always had a mind that doesn't know when to stop.

One problem becomes five questions. One mistake becomes ten possibilities. One conversation can replay in my head for hours.

Sometimes that helped me. It made me curious. It made me question how things worked. It made me dig deeper when everyone else was satisfied with the first answer.

But sometimes... my own thoughts became my biggest opponent.

I could overthink a simple decision until it felt impossible. I could doubt myself even after doing something right. And there were moments when I wondered:

"Am I actually good enough?"

I didn't always have an answer.

"SO I KEPT GOING ANYWAY."
03 COLLEGE · SREC TIRUPATI
B.TECH · ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE & DATA SCIENCE

Then came engineering.

A new environment. New people. New expectations. And a completely different level of pressure.

At first, I thought college would simply be about studying, getting good marks, learning programming, and eventually getting a job.

I was wrong.

College taught me something much bigger. It taught me about people. About friendship. Competition. Ego. Trust. Betrayal.

And sometimes... loneliness in a room full of people.

04 TURNING POINT

Then came Physics.

One subject. One exam. One result. And suddenly, something that looked small on paper felt enormous in my head.

I failed. I had to face a backlog.

For someone who wanted to prove himself academically, it hurt. There were people who laughed. People who made comments. People who made me feel like one failed subject somehow defined me.

For a while, I believed them. Maybe I wasn't good enough. Maybe I had fallen behind. Maybe everyone else was moving forward while I was stuck.

But eventually I understood something:

A backlog is a result. It isn't an identity.

So I went back. I wrote the exam again. And this time... I cleared it.

FALLEN ≠ FINISHED

The subject that once represented failure became one of the first lessons I carried forward:

Falling behind isn't the same as being finished.

05 PEOPLE & DISTANCE

Not everyone who stands beside you is standing with you.

Some friendships are real. Some are temporary. And some disappear the moment your interests stop matching theirs.

There were moments when I trusted people and later realized that the trust wasn't being returned. Decisions made without me. Credit taken. Things said behind my back. Sometimes people tried to make me feel smaller so they could feel bigger.

I didn't always know how to respond. Sometimes I argued. Sometimes I became angry. Sometimes I simply walked away.

Eventually I learned something important: Not every battle deserves your energy. Some people don't need an explanation. They need distance.

And sometimes walking away is not weakness. It's choosing peace.

"WALKING AWAY CAN ALSO BE MOVING FORWARD."
06 THE CONVERGENCE

Days when everything seemed to happen at once.

College. Assignments. Exams. Projects. Programming. Competitions. Expectations. Future. Career. Money. Family.

And the endless question: "What are you going to do with your life?"

I didn't have a perfect answer. I still don't.

But I started experimenting.

"SO I STARTED BUILDING."
07 & 08 THE WORKSHOP · LINUX & UBUNTU

My laptop stopped being just a laptop. It became my workshop.

I started learning. C. Python. Java. Linux. Git. GitHub. Cloud computing. Artificial intelligence. Web development. Robotics.

Sometimes I broke things. Then I fixed them. Sometimes I spent hours fighting a problem that eventually turned out to be one missing character.

Eventually, I made a decision that scared me a little: I left Windows behind and installed Ubuntu. A clean beginning. No safety net. Just me, Linux, and a terminal.

And somewhere along the way... I stopped being afraid of the terminal.

11 ROBOTICS & E-YANTRA

Teaching machines how to see and move.

Then came robotics. e-Yantra. A completely different world.

ROS. Python. OpenCV. Sensors. Simulation. Inverse kinematics. Robot movement. Debugging. And a ridiculous number of terminal commands.

Sometimes the robot didn't do what I expected. Sometimes the code worked yesterday and suddenly didn't work today. Sometimes I had absolutely no idea why something was failing.

But every failure forced me to understand the system better. I wasn't just writing code anymore. I was teaching machines how to perceive physical reality.

And that feeling... was different.

12 THE NIGHT HOURS

Some of my most important progress didn't happen in classrooms.

It happened late at night. A laptop glowing in a dark room.

Terminal open. Browser tabs everywhere. Documentation. GitHub. YouTube. AI assistants. One problem. Then another.

Sometimes I would tell myself: "Just five more minutes."

And suddenly it would be 2 AM. Not because I was forced to. Because I wanted the thing to work.

There is something addictive about solving a problem that refused to be solved. That moment when the error disappears. The program finally runs. The robot finally moves. The website finally loads.

You look at the screen and think:

14 THE MINDSET

The Real Competition.

For a long time, I thought my biggest competition was everyone around me. The student with better marks. The person who knew more code. The person who seemed more confident. The person who already had an internship.

Eventually I realized something:

My real competition was yesterday's version of me.

If I knew one more thing today... I had progressed.
If I fixed one bug... I had progressed.
If I understood one concept... I had progressed.
If I recovered from one bad day... I had progressed.

I used to think successful people were people who didn't fail. Now I think differently:

Maybe success is simply what happens when you fail... and don't stay there.

15 THE HORIZON

Still Not There.

I'm still not where I want to be. There are still things I don't know. Technologies I haven't mastered. Projects sitting unfinished.

There are still days when I doubt myself. Moments when I overthink. Failures I remember.

But something has changed: I no longer expect the road to be easy. I expect it to be difficult. And I'm okay with that.

I don't know exactly where this road ends. Maybe AI. Maybe software engineering. Maybe robotics. Maybe building a company. Maybe I'll fail at ten more things before I succeed at one.

And honestly... that's exciting.

"BECAUSE THE STORY ISN'T FINISHED."
